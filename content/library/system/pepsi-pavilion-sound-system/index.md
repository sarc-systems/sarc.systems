Designed by [David Tudor](/library/david-tudor/) as an "instrument" in its own right, working with [Gordon Mumma](/library/gordon-mumma/) and Fred Waldhauer, the [Pepsi Pavilion](/library/pepsi-pavilion/)'s sound system distributed audio across 37 speakers arranged in a rhombic grid behind the Pavilion's spherical mirror, driven by as many as 32 simultaneous inputs spanning the full audio-frequency range. Input sources included 16 quarter-track monaural tape recorders, 16 microphone preamplifiers, wave generators, and a record player; Tudor assembled 37 program tapes from a library of more than 500 collected sounds — insect calls, whale song, thunder, NASA recordings, and Smithsonian bird recordings among them — that supplied the system's raw material. Signals could be modulated (AM or FM), filtered, or left unchanged, then distributed to the dome's speakers in three distinct modes: line sound (rapid speaker-to-speaker switching), point sound (isolated single-speaker activation), and immersion sound (omnidirectional distribution across all 37 speakers). Distribution patterns could be pre-programmed or driven manually at the [Pepsi Pavilion Sound Modifier Console](/library/pepsi-pavilion-sound-modifier-console/), the system's playable control surface.
