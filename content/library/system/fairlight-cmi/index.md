Invented by Peter Vogel and Kim Ryrie in 1979, the Fairlight CMI combined a computer mainframe, music and typewriter keyboards, and a light-pen-driven monitor for "drawing" sound — the first polyphonic digital sampling synthesizer, used by Kate Bush, Brian Eno, and countless film and television composers, at a cost of $25,000–$75,000 across roughly 300 units built.
