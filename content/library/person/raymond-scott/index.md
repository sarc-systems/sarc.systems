Born Harry Warnow in Brooklyn in 1908, Raymond Scott adopted a pseudonym to lead his own ensembles alongside his bandleader brother, forming the six-piece Raymond Scott Quintette in 1936 and scoring novelty-jazz hits like "Powerhouse" and "The Toy Trumpet" — pieces Carl Stalling later adapted into over 120 Warner Bros. *Looney Tunes* and *Merrie Melodies* scores after Scott sold his publishing rights to the studio in 1943. Scott turned to electronics full-time with Manhattan Research, Inc., founded in 1946, building ring modulators, envelope shapers, and sequencers pursuing a lifelong project of automatic composition; his most ambitious device, the Electronium — by his own account eleven years and nearly a million dollars in the making — was a self-composing tone-and-rhythm generator. For the Clavivox, a ribbon-controlled keyboard synthesizer, Scott brought in a young [Robert Moog](/library/robert-moog/), who met him in the 1950s and designed circuits for him through the 1960s. In 1971 Berry Gordy hired Scott as director of Motown's electronic music and research department, a post he held until 1977.
