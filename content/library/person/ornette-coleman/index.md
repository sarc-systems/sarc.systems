Ornette Coleman (1930–2015, Fort Worth, Texas) established free jazz as a distinct practice with *The Shape of Jazz to Come* (1959) and the twin-quartet recording *Free Jazz: A Collective Improvisation* (1960), moving harmonic and rhythmic structure away from fixed chord changes toward collective, real-time invention. Through the 1970s he elaborated this into [harmolodics](/library/harmolodics/), a formal theory placing harmony, melody, and rhythm on equal footing, developed further with his electric group Prime Time on albums including *Dancing in Your Head* (1976). His *Sound Grammar* (2006) won the 2007 Pulitzer Prize for Music.
