Jorge Luis Borges (1899–1986) worked from 1938 as first assistant at the Miguel Cané Municipal Library in Buenos Aires, cataloging some hundred books a day, and by 1955 — the year his eyesight failed completely — had been appointed director of Argentina's National Library. His two major collections, *Ficciones* (1944) and *El Aleph* (1949), gathered short fictions built on infinite libraries, forking labyrinths, and worlds that overwrite reality through language alone: *[The Library of Babel](/library/the-library-of-babel/)* imagines the universe as a total archive of every possible book of fixed length, and *[Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius](/library/tlon-uqbar-orbis-tertius/)* traces an invented encyclopedia's slow takeover of the real world. Shared recognition of the 1961 Prix Formentor, awarded jointly with Samuel Beckett, brought him international attention and helped establish the Latin American Boom.
