Hugh Davies (1943–2005) studied music at Worcester College, Oxford, from 1961 to 1964, then moved to Cologne to become [Karlheinz Stockhausen](/library/karlheinz-stockhausen/)'s personal assistant, assembling and documenting material for his compositions and performing in his live ensemble. Davies went on to build experimental instruments from ordinary household objects, most notably the shozyg — a generic term for instruments built inside book covers, the first housed in an encyclopedia volume spanning "SHO–" to "ZYG–." He played live electronics in the [Music Improvisation Company](/library/music-improvisation-company/) from 1968 to 1971 and in the ensemble Gentle Fire from 1968 to 1975, founded and directed Goldsmiths, University of London's Electronic Music Studios from 1968 to 1986, and published a comprehensive international electronic-music catalog in 1968. He later appeared on Talk Talk's 1988 album *Spirit of Eden*.
