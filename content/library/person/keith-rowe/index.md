Keith Rowe (b. 1940, Plymouth, England) began playing jazz guitar in the early 1960s before abandoning conventional technique — resolving one New Year's not to tune his guitar again — to lay the instrument flat on a table and manipulate its strings, body, and pickups with objects including alligator clips, a library card, springs, and hand-held electric fans. A founding member of [AMM](/library/amm/) in 1965, he developed this "tabletop guitar" practice into one of the foundational vocabularies of free improvisation, later extending it with radio static and electronics. Trained as a visual artist, he has used his own paintings on many of his album covers. Widely regarded as a central figure — sometimes called a godfather — of electroacoustic improvisation (EAI), he recorded extensively for Erstwhile Records, including long-running duo collaborations with Toshimaru Nakamura beginning with *Weather Sky* (2001).
