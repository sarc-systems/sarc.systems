John Fahey (1939–2001) built what became known as "American Primitive" guitar, fusing blues fingerpicking learned from old 78s with the dissonance of twentieth-century classical composers such as Béla Bartók and Ralph Vaughan Williams. After a 1994 *Spin* profile drove a rediscovery of his work, Fahey turned toward the avant-garde in a late-career resurgence: [Jim O'Rourke](/library/jim-orourke/) produced his 1997 album *Womblife*, part of what commentators describe as Fahey's collaborative turn toward experimental music in that period. In 1996, using an inheritance from his father, Fahey co-founded [Revenant Records](/library/revenant-records/) with Dean Blackwood to reissue obscure blues, old-time, and experimental recordings.
