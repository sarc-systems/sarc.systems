Suzanne Ciani (b. 1946) studied composition at Wellesley College and, during a master's degree at UC Berkeley (1968–1970), met [Don Buchla](/library/research/don-buchla/) and became one of the earliest and most enduring performers on his modular instruments. She worked at [Buchla & Associates](/library/research/buchla-and-associates/), soldering components to earn money toward her own Buchla 200 system, then moved to New York in 1974 and built a career threading avant-garde electronic composition through commercial sound design: she designed audio logos for Fortune 500 companies, most famously Coca-Cola's "pop and pour" sound, and in 1980 created the voice and electronic effects — processed through a vocoder — for Bally's Xenon pinball machine, the first human female voice in a pinball game. Her 1982 debut album *Seven Waves* and five subsequent Grammy-nominated albums (1988–2000) established her as a major voice in electronic and new-age composition. She founded her own label, Seventh Wave, in 1994, and has continued performing live on Buchla systems into the 2010s and 2020s, including 2016's *Buchla Concerts 1975* and 2018's *Live Quadraphonic*, her first solo Buchla performance in forty years.
