Klaus Schulze (1947–2022) drummed on [Tangerine Dream](/library/research/tangerine-dream/)'s 1970 debut *Electronic Meditation* before leaving to co-found Ash Ra Tempel with Manuel Göttsching in 1970, departing after its one self-titled album to begin a solo career that ran to more than 60 releases across six decades. His debut, *[Irrlicht](/library/research/irrlicht/)* (1972), built from organ and a heavily filtered orchestral recording, dispensed with synthesizers almost entirely and is widely regarded as a founding document of the "Berlin School" of electronic music; *[Cyborg](/library/research/cyborg/)* (1973) added the EMS VCS3, and later albums such as *Timewind* (1975), *Moondawn* (1976), and *[Mirage](/library/research/mirage-klaus-schulze/)* (1977) established the sequencer-driven, side-long electronic form the label came to describe. From 1994 he collaborated extensively with [Pete Namlook](/library/research/pete-namlook/) on FAX, beginning with *[The Dark Side of the Moog](/library/research/the-dark-side-of-the-moog/)*, a series that grew to eleven volumes released between 1994 and 2008. Fans and critics alike came to call him the "Godfather of Trance" for the influence of his hypnotic, sequenced electronic music on the genre.
