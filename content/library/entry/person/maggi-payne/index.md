Maggi Payne (b. 1945, Temple, Texas, raised in Amarillo) began on flute at nine and was given a Webcor reel-to-reel by her father, a doctor with a taste for gadgets, at ten — an early pairing of music and machine she never really left. She took a B.Mus. in flute at Northwestern before an M.Mus. at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign, where she studied with [Gordon Mumma](/library/research/gordon-mumma/), Ben Johnston, and Salvatore Martirano and crossed paths with computer-music pioneers [Lejaren Hiller](/library/research/lejaren-hiller/) and [Herbert Brün](/library/research/herbert-brun/) — along with a single, much-remembered lunch conversation with [John Cage](/library/research/john-cage/). She moved to [Mills College](/library/research/mills-center-for-contemporary-music/) in 1970 for its newly established electronic music program, earning an MFA under [Robert Ashley](/library/research/robert-ashley/)'s mentorship. Joining the Mills College Center for Contemporary Music in 1972 and co-directing it from 1992 to 2018, she taught recording engineering, composition, and electronic music for more than fifty years, working across tape, synthesis, and video in her own compositions — early pieces such as *Hum* (1973) explored spatialization across multiple microphones, while later ones like *Crystal* (1986) paired sound with her own photomicrography of crystal growth and NASA solar-wind data. She contributed electric flute to [David Behrman](/library/research/david-behrman/)'s segment of Ashley's documentary *Music with Roots in the Aether* (1976) and to his album *On the Other Ocean* (1977), and shared the Mills faculty for years with [Terry Riley](/library/research/terry-riley/) and synthesizer designer Don Buchla. From the 1980s she collaborated with video artist Ed Tannenbaum on his *Technological Feets* live dance/video works, and built a flame speaker at San Francisco's Exploratorium with Nick Bertoni (1983–85).
