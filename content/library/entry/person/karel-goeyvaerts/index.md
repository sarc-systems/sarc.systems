Karel Goeyvaerts (1923–1993) met [Karlheinz Stockhausen](/library/research/karlheinz-stockhausen/) at the 1951 Darmstadt summer courses, and the two — both devout Catholics who folded numerological ideas into serial technique — became close collaborators in total serialism's earliest years. When Stockhausen began work at the [Studio für elektronische Musik des WDR](/library/research/studio-fur-elektronische-musik-des-wdr/) in Cologne in July 1953, he helped Goeyvaerts realize *Compositie Nr. 5 met zuivere tonen* ("Composition No. 5 with Pure Tones"), one of the studio's first sine-tone electronic pieces. After withdrawing from public musical life for much of the 1960s, Goeyvaerts accepted a position in 1970 at [IPEM](/library/research/ipem/) in Ghent, where he taught composition with computers, later synthesizing his serial background with minimalist technique in works including the opera *Aquarius* (1983–92).
