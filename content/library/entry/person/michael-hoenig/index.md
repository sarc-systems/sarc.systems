Michael Hoenig (b. 1952, Hamburg) joined the Berlin electronic group Agitation Free in 1971, contributing synthesizer treatments through albums including *Malesch* (1972) before, in 1975, briefly replacing Peter Baumann in [Tangerine Dream](/library/research/tangerine-dream/) for an Australian tour and a BBC-recorded Royal Albert Hall concert. His sole 1970s solo album, *[Departure From the Northern Wasteland](/library/research/departure-from-the-northern-wasteland/)* (1978), drew directly on that Tangerine Dream lineage — built from sequenced analog synthesizer patterns close in spirit to the Froese-Baumann-Franke Berlin School sound, with guitar contributed by former Agitation Free bandmates Lutz Ulbrich and Michael "Micki" Duwe. Hoenig relocated to Los Angeles shortly after its release and became a prolific film and television composer through his company Metamusic Productions, receiving a Primetime Emmy nomination for the 1997 series *Dark Skies* and later scoring the *Baldur's Gate* video games.
