Born April 12, 1940, in Chicago, Herbie Hancock brought Moog modular synthesis into jazz in the early 1970s through his Mwandishi sextet, initially hiring synthesist [Patrick Gleeson](/library/research/patrick-gleeson/) as a studio technician for *Crossings* (1972) and *[Sextant](/library/research/sextant/)* (1973) before asking him to join full-time as the group expanded to a septet. Hancock returned to electronic production on *Sunlight* (1978) and, on 1983's *Future Shock*, the Grammy-winning single "Rockit" — co-written with Bill Laswell and Michael Beinhorn, with scratching by GrandMixer D.ST — became an early landmark of instrumental hip-hop and electro crossover.
