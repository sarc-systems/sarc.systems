Born November 9, 1934, Patrick Gleeson taught 18th-century English literature at San Francisco State University before leaving academia for music full-time; hearing [Wendy Carlos](/library/research/wendy-carlos/)'s *Switched-On Bach* (1968) led him to take up a Moog III modular. He co-founded Different Fur Recording Studio in San Francisco's Mission District and engineered [Terry Riley](/library/research/terry-riley/)'s *[Persian Surgery Dervishes](/library/research/persian-surgery-dervishes/)* (1972), recorded live in Los Angeles and Paris. Separately, hired by [Herbie Hancock](/library/research/herbie-hancock/) as a synthesizer technician for *Crossings* (1972) and *[Sextant](/library/research/sextant/)* (1973), he was soon asked to join Hancock's Mwandishi band full-time, touring on an ARP 2600 and pioneering live synthesizer performance in jazz. He later scored film work, including leading the synthesizer team on *Apocalypse Now*, and continued releasing solo electronic recordings until his death on July 19, 2026, at 91.
