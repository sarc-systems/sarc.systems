A device that automatically regulates a machine's operating speed, using the machine's own output as the signal that corrects it — the earliest widely cited example of engineered feedback control. James Watt adapted the centrifugal (flyball) governor to steam engines from 1788 onward, using a spinning pair of weights whose rise and fall with engine speed throttles the steam supply to hold speed steady. [Norbert Wiener](/library/research/norbert-wiener/) took the Greek word for "governor" or "steersman," *kybernetes*, as the root of "cybernetics" in 1948, making the governor's self-correcting loop the namesake of the whole field.
