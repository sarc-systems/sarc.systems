A grid of cells, each holding one of a small set of states, updated simultaneously across the whole grid by a simple rule that looks only at each cell's immediate neighbors — from which strikingly complex, often unpredictable global patterns can emerge from purely local rules. [John von Neumann](/library/research/john-von-neumann/) developed the concept in the late 1940s, working from a lattice formalism suggested by Stanislaw Ulam, in pursuit of a mathematically rigorous self-reproducing automaton. [John Conway](/library/research/john-conway/)'s Game of Life, published by Martin Gardner in *Scientific American* in October 1970, remains the best-known example.
