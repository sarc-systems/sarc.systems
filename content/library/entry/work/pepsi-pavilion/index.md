The Pepsi Pavilion was an artist-designed and artist-programmed environment created for PepsiCo by Experiments in Art and Technology (E.A.T.) at [Expo '70](/library/research/expo-70/) in Osaka. Its core creative team — Robert Breer, Frosty Myers, Robert Whitman, and [David Tudor](/library/research/david-tudor/) — was joined by [Gordon Mumma](/library/research/gordon-mumma/), Lowell Cross, Fujiko Nakaya, Tony Martin, Ardison Phillips, and [Pauline Oliveros](/library/research/pauline-oliveros/), with architect John Pierce, to build a dome-shaped structure fronted by a 90-foot spherical mirror. Inside, Tony Martin's programmable lighting and Fujiko Nakaya's fog installation combined with a purpose-built multichannel sound environment (see the [Pepsi Pavilion Sound System](/library/research/pepsi-pavilion-sound-system/)) and, in its Clam Room, a sound-activated laser deflection system built by Tudor and Lowell Cross — together making the Pavilion less a conventional exhibit than a playable instrument its visitors and resident artists could occupy and operate.
