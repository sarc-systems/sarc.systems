Published in France in 1968 as *Différence et répétition* (Deleuze's doctoral thesis), *Difference and Repetition* argues against treating difference as merely a deviation from prior identity or resemblance — difference, for Deleuze, is prior and generative, with identity itself a secondary effect produced by repeated difference. Repetition is correspondingly reconceived as productive of the new rather than a return of the same. The book supplies the philosophical groundwork — multiplicity, intensity, the virtual — that Deleuze's later collaborations with [Félix Guattari](/library/research/felix-guattari/) build on. Translated into English by Paul Patton (Columbia University Press, 1994).
