Published in *Nautilus* on 17 April 2017 under the subtitle "Where did language come from?", with an introductory note by David Krakauer, president of the [Santa Fe Institute](/library/entry/santa-fe-institute/), recalling two decades of conversations with McCarthy about the unconscious mind. The essay takes its title from August Kekulé's account of arriving at the ring structure of benzene through a dream of a snake seizing its own tail, and asks why the unconscious — a biological system that guided animal behavior long before speech existed — delivers its solutions as images, dreams, and stories rather than in plain language. McCarthy's answer is that language is a recent arrival in human evolution, one that spread through the brain "very much like a parasitic invasion" without being designed for it, and that the far older unconscious has never learned to speak in it.
