The Santa Fe Institute was founded in 1984 by a group of scientists, many associated with Los Alamos National Laboratory, led by chemist George Cowan, its first president. An independent, nonprofit, deliberately department-less institution, it became a central home for complexity science — the study of how adaptive systems in biology, physics, computation, economics, and society produce emergent order — drawing physicists, biologists, economists, and computer scientists into shared work. [Christopher Langton](/library/entry/christopher-langton/) continued his artificial-life research there, and novelist [Cormac McCarthy](/library/entry/cormac-mccarthy/) was a longtime trustee and fellow.
