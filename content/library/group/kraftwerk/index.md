Ralf Hütter and Florian Schneider founded Kraftwerk in Düsseldorf in 1970, out of an earlier group, Organisation, and an art-school milieu that also produced [Neu!](/library/neu/)'s Klaus Dinger and Michael Rother, both briefly members of an early Kraftwerk lineup before leaving to form their own group in 1971. Kraftwerk's classic quartet — Hütter, Schneider, and electronic percussionists Wolfgang Flür (from 1973) and Karl Bartos (from 1975) — built a run of concept albums voicing the machine culture of postwar Germany directly through synthesizers and vocoder: *Autobahn* (1974), a Top 5 US hit; *Radio-Activity* (1975); *Trans-Europe Express* (1977); *The Man-Machine* (1978); and *Computer World* (1981), which produced the single "The Model." Recording and performing as uniform-clad "robots" onstage as early as 1978, the group treated its own image as part of the composition. Its steady, minimal electronic pulse became a direct source for Detroit techno's Belleville Three and for early hip-hop (notably Afrika Bambaataa's "Planet Rock"), and its influence runs through synth-pop, house, and electronic music broadly.
