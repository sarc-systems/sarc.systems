Klaus Dinger had drummed on [Kraftwerk](/library/kraftwerk/)'s 1970 debut album, and Michael Rother briefly joined the group's touring lineup the following year; when that transitional, "floating" version of Kraftwerk dissolved in mid-1971, the two left together to form Neu! in Düsseldorf, recording their debut with producer Conny Plank at Hamburg's Star-Musik studio that December. Across *Neu!* (1972), *Neu! 2* (1973), and *Neu! '75* (1975), the duo pushed a sparer, more hypnotic and repetitive electric-guitar-and-drums sound than Kraftwerk's own contemporaneous, increasingly synthesizer-based direction. The steady, forward-driving "motorik" beat Dinger developed on these records — most audibly on "Hallogallo" — became one of the most widely cited rhythmic templates in krautrock and its later influence on post-punk and electronic music. The *Neu!* track "Negativland" gave the American tape-collage group [Negativland](/library/negativland/) its name and, in turn, the name of its own Seeland record label.
