George Orwell (Eric Arthur Blair, 1903–1950) drew on his own experience of wartime propaganda — at the BBC and in Spain — to build *[1984](/library/nineteen-eighty-four/)* (1949), a dystopia in which a state-controlled language, Newspeak, is engineered to make certain thoughts literally unformulable, and the past is continuously rewritten to match present political need. "Newspeak," "doublethink," and "Big Brother" passed from the novel into ordinary English as working descriptions of language used to constrain thought rather than express it.
