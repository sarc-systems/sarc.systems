Built by Soviet engineer Yevgeny Murzin between 1937 and 1957, the ANS synthesizer is named for composer Alexander Nikolayevich Scriabin, whose sound-color ideas inspired it. It generates sound photoelectronically: microtonal tracks are scratched directly onto five rotating glass discs (144 tracks each, 720 microtones in total), which a light source and photocells then read and convert to audio — a form of drawn, graphical sound distinct from Oskar Fischinger's or Norman McLaren's film-based methods. Housed in a dedicated Moscow studio, it was used by composers including Alfred Schnittke, Edison Denisov, and Sofia Gubaidulina, and by Eduard Artemyev on Andrei Tarkovsky's film scores, notably *Solaris* (1972); maintained for decades by engineer Stanislav Kreichi, it later drew [Coil](/library/coil/) to Russia in 2002 to record the instrument, released as the CD *ANS* (2003) and the expanded box set *COILANS* (2004).
