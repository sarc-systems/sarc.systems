Founded in 1951 at Nordwestdeutscher Rundfunk (later Westdeutscher Rundfunk) in Cologne after discussions between Herbert Eimert, Werner Meyer-Eppler, and Robert Beyer, the Studio für elektronische Musik des WDR was the world's first purpose-built electronic music studio. Eimert directed it from the start; Karlheinz Stockhausen took over direction in 1963, by which point the studio's alumni also included Gottfried Michael Koenig and Bruno Maderna. Stockhausen composed *Gesang der Jünglinge* (1956) and *Kontakte* (1960) there, the latter built partly around a rotating table of his own design; sound engineer Volker Müller, who joined in the 1970s, maintained the studio's equipment — including a custom-built Lawo mixing console, Telefunken Magnetophon 15A tape recorders, and an [EMS Synthi 100](/library/ems-synthi-100/) with vocoder — and its documentation through decades of relocation, first within Cologne in the 1980s and again to a basement location in 2001, before the collection was transferred to Stockhausen's birthplace, Haus Mödrath, by 2019.
