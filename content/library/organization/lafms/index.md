Founded in 1973 by Chip Chapman, Joe Potts, Rick Potts, and Tom Recchion, the Los Angeles Free Music Society (LAFMS) was a loose collective rather than a band — a shared umbrella under which member groups including Le Forte Four, Airway, [Smegma](/library/smegma/), Monitor, and Doo-Doettes self-released records, staged performances, and published the magazine *Light Bulb*, working outside any recognizable rock or jazz idiom. The group's own Poobah Records pressed its early LPs in tiny runs, later gathered on the ten-CD retrospective *The Lowest Form of Music* (1996). [John Duncan](/library/john-duncan/) was closely associated with the collective from 1978, working with Recchion, Fredrik Nilsen, and Joe Potts. Airway, dormant since the late 1970s, was revived for occasional performances from 1998 onward; [John Wiese](/library/john-wiese/) later joined that lineup alongside original members. LAFMS's DIY irreverence — closer to Dada and Wildman Fischer than any noise orthodoxy — is widely cited as an influence on later American art-damage groups and on Japanese noise acts including Hijokaidan and Incapacitants; the collective's work was the subject of a 2010 exhibition in London and a 2012 exhibition in Los Angeles.
