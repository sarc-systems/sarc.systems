W. Ross Ashby (1903–1972) was an English psychiatrist who became, after [Norbert Wiener](/library/norbert-wiener/), cybernetics' major theoretician. In 1948 he built the *homeostat*, an electromechanical device that demonstrated self-regulating, adaptive behavior, described in *Design for a Brain* (1952). *An Introduction to Cybernetics* (1956) gave the field its first systematic mathematical treatment and introduced the Law of Requisite Variety — only variety can destroy variety, so a regulator can reduce a system's disorder only to the extent that the regulator itself possesses at least as much variety in its own possible responses. Ashby was also a member of London's Ratio Club (1949–1958), the informal group of British cyberneticists that included [Alan Turing](/library/alan-turing/) and Grey Walter.
