Julian Jaynes (1920–1997) worked at Yale and then, from 1964 to 1995, as a research associate at Princeton, after an early detour as an actor and playwright in England. His 1976 book *[The Origin of Consciousness in the Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind](/library/research/origin-of-consciousness/)* argued that introspective consciousness is not innate but arises from language and metaphor, and is historically recent — that earlier humans instead operated in a "bicameral" mode, experiencing the commands of one brain hemisphere as external, auditory-hallucinated voices of gods or kings. He drew his evidence largely from close reading of the *Iliad* and other early texts for signs of this shift in mentality during the second millennium BCE.
