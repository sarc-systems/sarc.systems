Steve Lacy (1934–2004) was the first avant-garde jazz musician to specialize in the soprano saxophone, single-handedly returning the instrument to modern music after playing with Cecil Taylor, Gil Evans, and Thelonious Monk. In 1963 his quartet with Roswell Rudd, Henry Grimes, and [Denis Charles](/library/research/denis-charles/) recorded a live set of Monk repertoire, released in 1975 as *[School Days](/library/research/school-days-1963/)*. From the 1980s on he formed a close duo partnership with pianist [Mal Waldron](/library/research/mal-waldron/), documented with [Andrew Cyrille](/library/research/andrew-cyrille/) and Reggie Workman on *[The Mighty Warriors: Live in Antwerp](/library/research/the-mighty-warriors-live-in-antwerp/)* (recorded 1995). Settling for a period in Rome after 1965, he began performing with Musica Elettronica Viva's Alvin Curran and Frederic Rzewski in the group's late-1960s improvisations.
