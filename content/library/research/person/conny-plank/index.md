Konrad "Conny" Plank (1940–1987) began engineering and producing in the German underground scene of the late 1960s, building a custom 56-channel mixing desk by hand for the studio he ran near Cologne from the early 1970s on. He recorded [Kraftwerk](/library/research/kraftwerk/)'s earliest albums, engineered [Neu!](/library/research/neu/)'s self-titled 1972 debut at Hamburg's Star-Musik studio, and went on to produce [Cluster](/library/research/cluster/) and [Harmonia](/library/research/harmonia/) as well as Can, Guru Guru, and La Düsseldorf — a body of work that, more than any single artist's, defines the sound now called krautrock. His studio also hosted Brian Eno's sessions with Cluster and Harmonia in the mid-1970s, and he recorded directly with [Dieter Moebius](/library/research/dieter-moebius/) as Moebius & Plank, joined by Guru Guru drummer Mani Neumeier on 1983's *[Zero Set](/library/research/zero-set/)*. Plank later carried his live, hybrid-instrumentation production approach into new wave, producing Ultravox, Eurythmics, D.A.F., and Killing Joke.
