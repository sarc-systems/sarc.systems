Born in Wichita, Kansas, in 1953, John Duncan studied under Allan Kaprow at CalArts before becoming closely associated with the [Los Angeles Free Music Society](/library/research/lafms/) from 1978, working alongside Tom Recchion, Fredrik Nilsen, and Joe Potts. His performance and installation work — including *Scare* (1976), *Blind Date* (1980), and *The Keening Towers* — uses shortwave radio, field recordings, and confrontational staging to work directly on an audience's perception and nervous system rather than presenting sound as music to be listened to at a remove. Duncan later became a formative link between American and Japanese noise scenes, releasing *The Black Album* with [Merzbow](/library/research/merzbow/) and collaborating with Keiji Haino, Cosey Fanni Tutti, and Chris Carter. He has lived in Bologna, Italy, since the 1990s.
