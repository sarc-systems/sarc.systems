Serialism organizes musical material — pitch, and potentially rhythm, dynamics, or timbre — into an ordered series that governs a composition's structure, rather than relying on traditional tonal harmony. Arnold Schoenberg introduced its founding form, twelve-tone technique, in the 1920s: a fixed row using all twelve chromatic pitches, related only to one another, providing melodic and harmonic unity without a tonal center. Anton Webern and Alban Berg extended the method within Schoenberg's Second Viennese School, and it became foundational to postwar composers including Pierre Boulez, [Karlheinz Stockhausen](/library/karlheinz-stockhausen/), and Luigi Nono, who extended it toward [total serialism](/library/total-serialism/).
