Founded by Edgar Froese in West Berlin in 1967, Tangerine Dream's early "Pink Years" lineup included [Conrad Schnitzler](/library/conrad-schnitzler/) on cello, violin, and effects from 1969 to 1970, contributing to the group's debut *Electronic Meditation* (1970) before leaving to found Kluster with Dieter Moebius and Hans-Joachim Roedelius. Tangerine Dream continued through further electroacoustic albums — *Alpha Centauri* (1971), *Zeit* (1972), *Atem* (1973) — before the mid-1970s trio of Froese, Christopher Franke, and Peter Baumann established the sequencer-driven "Berlin School" sound on *Phaedra* (1974), a UK top-20 hit for Virgin Records. The group became prolific film composers from the late 1970s onward and continued recording and touring after Froese's death in 2015 under bandleader Thorsten Quaeschning.
