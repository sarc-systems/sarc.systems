Formed in Concord, California in 1979 by high-school students Mark Hosler and Richard Lyons, Negativland took its name — and, later, the name of its own Seeland record label — from "Negativland," a track on [Neu!](/library/neu/)'s 1972 debut album. The group built a body of work — beginning with their 1980 debut LP — from tape collage, hoax, and broadcast interception. Radio host Don Joyce, who joined soon after and ran the long-running KPFA program *Over the Edge*, introduced the term "culture jammer" on the group's 1984 album *JamCon '84*. Their 1991 single "U2" paired kazoo parodies of "I Still Haven't Found What I'm Looking For" with an obscenity-laced outtake of DJ Casey Kasem losing his temper during a jingle session; Island Records sued over the packaging, arguing that "U2" printed larger than "Negativland" passed the record off as U2's own, and a separate falling-out with their own label, SST Records — whose founder released a live album against the band's wishes — severed that relationship entirely. Negativland documented the whole episode, together with essays on copyright and fair use, in the 1995 book-and-CD *Fair Use: The Story of the Letter U and the Numeral 2*.
