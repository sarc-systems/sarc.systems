Introduced in 1970 as ARP's first synthesizer, the modular ARP 2500 arrived after Moog's and Buchla's earliest instruments but drew attention for a comparatively rare feature at the time: it held its tuning without drifting. Only around 100 units were built, with an estimated 50 surviving in working order; many were bought not by music departments but by university science departments as a demonstration instrument for analog electronics, including one still used for teaching at the University of Virginia's Center for Computer Music. [Éliane Radigue](/library/research/eliane-radigue/) worked with her own 2500 from 1974 to 2000, and a fully loaded unit with two wing cabinets and dual keyboards appeared on the set of *Close Encounters of the Third Kind* (1977); a surviving unit is on display at the National Music Centre in Calgary.
