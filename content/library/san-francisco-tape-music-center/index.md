Growing out of composer Ramon Sender's "Sonics" concert series at the San Francisco Conservatory (first performed December 1961, featuring Sender, Pauline Oliveros, and Terry Riley among others), the San Francisco Tape Music Center was founded in the summer of 1962 by Sender and Morton Subotnick. Pauline Oliveros, artist Tony Martin, and technician William Maginnis soon joined, and the collective commissioned engineer Don Buchla to build the Buchla 100, among the first modular voltage-controlled synthesizers. In 1966 the Center accepted a grant to join Mills College as the Mills Center for Contemporary Music, with Oliveros as founding director.
