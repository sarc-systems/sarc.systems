David Hilbert (1862–1943) championed Georg Cantor's set theory against its early critics — "No one shall expel us from the paradise that Cantor has created," he wrote — and built his own foundational program, an axiomatic formalization of mathematics, partly in its defense. Working at Göttingen, where a young [John von Neumann](/library/john-von-neumann/) studied under him in the 1920s, Hilbert posed the *Entscheidungsproblem* (decision problem) in 1928 with Wilhelm Ackermann, asking whether an algorithm could decide the truth of any mathematical statement — a question Alan Turing answered negatively in 1936 by defining the Turing machine.
