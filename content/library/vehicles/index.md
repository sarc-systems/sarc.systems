In a sequence of thought experiments, Braitenberg builds up imaginary
"vehicles" from a few sensors, motors, and wires and shows how strikingly
lifelike behavior — fear, aggression, love, foresight — emerges from
arrangements that contain none of those things. It is a study in *synthetic*
psychology: understanding by constructing, from the simplest coupling of
perception to action upward.

The book argues that rich behavior is a property of *organization* — of coupling and feedback
between simple parts — rather than of complex components; and that the way to
understand such systems is to build them and watch what they do.
