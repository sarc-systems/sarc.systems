Established in 1964 after physicist James Van Allen, supervising student James Cessna's project to build one of the world's first digital synthesizers, approached composition head Philip Bezanson about housing it, the University of Iowa Electronic Music Studios were among the first in the world with real-time digital synthesis. Robert Shallenberg directed the Studios alongside Cessna's ongoing work on the instrument; Kenneth Gaburo, appointed director in 1983, expanded the Studios into speech, text, and image manipulation.
