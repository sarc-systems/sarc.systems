Stanisław Lem (1921–2006) trained in medicine in Lwów and Kraków before turning to fiction, building a body of work that used science fiction as an instrument for genuine philosophical and technical speculation rather than adventure. His 1964 treatise *Summa Technologiae* worked through prospective cybernetic, biological, and information-theoretic futures — virtual environments and molecular-scale engineering among them — decades ahead of those fields' emergence. The 1971 story *[Non Serviam](/library/non-serviam/)*, framed as a review of a (fictional) book on "personetics," follows computer-simulated "personoids" reasoning their way toward a theology of their own creator, one of several Lem fictions built directly on the era's cybernetic and information-theoretic vocabulary.
