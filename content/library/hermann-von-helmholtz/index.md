Hermann von Helmholtz (1821–1894) held professorships in physiology and physics at Königsberg, Bonn, Heidelberg, and finally Berlin, working across optics, thermodynamics, and electromagnetism alongside his acoustics research. His 1863 *[On the Sensations of Tone](/library/on-the-sensations-of-tone/)* built the Helmholtz resonator to isolate and measure individual partials, connecting the physics of vibrating bodies to the physiology of the ear and to musical consonance and dissonance — a psychophysical method, relating measurable stimulus to perceived experience, that shaped acoustics and music theory for the following century. [Harry Partch](/library/harry-partch/) discovered a translation of the book in 1923 and credited it as the discovery that turned him away from twelve-tone equal temperament toward just intonation.
